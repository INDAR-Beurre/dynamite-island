## 10. INFERENCES only — mapped to our suspected defects

These are **my** applications of his stated rules, not things he said. They are deliberately hedged.

- **Teal glow/bloom + multi-stop gradient on dictation.** His *stated* "you only get one" accent, "the only saturated color in the whole palette", "this layer's job is to be quiet" and "looking at something static" point away from an animated saturated bloom on a secondary state. **INFERENCE:** likely contrary to his taste. But he never mentions glow, bloom, gradients in UI, dictation, or waveforms, so this is a taste judgement, not a quoted rule. The reference video's absence of waveform/glow/gradient (owner's observation) is consistent with the inference.
- **Workspace-disc drop shadow while the pill has none.** **INFERENCE:** his "contrast is basically deciding where people look first" suggests secondary indicators should not out-shout the primary capsule; a shadow on discs but not the pill could misdirect the budget. He says **nothing** about shadows — weak inference, do not treat as a stated rule.
- **Invisible rim-light constant.** **INFERENCE:** "every gap every color every corner radius in your rice Is a decision that you made" argues against retaining an effect that measures as invisible, but he gives no rule about invisible effects. No direct support.
- **Accent hue hand-typed in four files.** Strongest match to an actual statement: "you set up the palette once with a color for each job… all of it pulls from that same palette… you know exactly why every color on your screen is there." **INFERENCE:** centralise the accent. This is a workflow statement from him, applied to our file layout.
- **Clock-first resting capsule.** Direct statement support in §1 and §3, not an inference.
- **Panel expanding out of the button.** Direct statement support in §4, not an inference.
